After Midnight: How the 2026 U.S.–Canada Talks Collapsed

Carney suspends talks, midnight Section 338 duties, dollar-for-dollar from Sept. 8

Ebook · $2.99 USD · L. Merrick

Minutes before a midnight customs clock on Friday, August 21, 2026, Prime Minister Mark Carney suspended U.S.–Canada trade talks and sent negotiators back to Ottawa. His newswire statement said last-minute U.S. terms were “unfair, uneconomic, and called into question the reliability of any deal,” and that the United States intended a 50 percent tariff on “roughly $28 billion of Canadian goods.” USTR’s working number is about $20 billion. Canada would match “dollar for dollar.”

This book is the 72-hour talk-collapse file—not the duty mechanism. (That companion brief is The Neighbor Tax.) It sets Carney’s Friday text and Saturday press conference (“They asked too much and offered too little”; “You’re at war when you get attacked”) beside Jamieson Greer’s “Canada declined to finalize the trade deal under the terms agreed earlier this week,” the Fox News line that no new talks were planned, and the September 8 Canadian counter-tariff date. Sources: PMO/newswire.ca, Reuters as carried by CNBC and ABC, BBC, CNBC, NBC News, Al Jazeera. No invented quotes. No faces on the cover. No ASIN until one exists.
