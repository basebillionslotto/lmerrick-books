Spain’s Second Crown: How La Roja Took the 2026 World Cup

A sourced chronicle of the 2026 FIFA World Cup winners

Ebook · $2.99 USD · L. Merrick

On July 19, 2026, Spain won the men’s World Cup for the second time. The score in East Rutherford, New Jersey, was 1–0 after extra time. Ferran Torres scored in the 106th minute. Argentina, the defending champions, finished with ten players.

This independent digital chronicle reconstructs that title from FIFA match reports and from Associated Press, Reuters, and ESPN dispatches filed during the tournament. It follows Luis de la Fuente’s side from a goalless opening draw with Cabo Verde in Atlanta through a 4–0 against Saudi Arabia, a 1–0 in Guadalajara, knockout wins over Austria, Portugal, Belgium, and France, and the extra-time final at MetLife Stadium.

The book is sports history, not gossip. Quotations are limited to remarks already published by FIFA or the wires. No locker-room dialogue is invented. Individual awards—Rodri’s Golden Ball, Unai Simón’s Golden Glove, Pau Cubarsí’s Young Player Award—are taken from FIFA’s July 19 honors list. Records (one goal conceded, seven clean sheets, the oldest winning coach) are cited to those same sources.

FIFA and Spain appear only as the names of the public tournament and the national team. This is not an official FIFA, UEFA, or RFEF product.
