Fifty Years of U.S. Immigration Policy: Statutes and Presidents, 1975–2026

A sourced documentary history of IRCA, the 1990 Act, IIRIRA, DHS, DACA, and the administrations that applied them

Ebook · $3.99 USD · L. Merrick

From the fall of Saigon to the Laken Riley Act, U.S. immigration policy has been rewritten more often by public laws and court opinions than by slogans. This concise documentary history follows the statutes and the presidents who signed, implemented, or worked around them—from 1975 through August 2026.

L. Merrick explains, in plain language and without a party brief, how the Immigration and Nationality Act still sits at the center of the system; how IRCA 1986 combined legalization and employer sanctions; how the Immigration Act of 1990 rebuilt legal ceilings, the diversity lottery, and temporary-worker caps; and how IIRIRA 1996 created today’s unlawful-presence bars, expedited removal, and mandatory detention. The book then traces the post-9/11 creation of DHS, USCIS, ICE, and CBP; the rise of executive tools after comprehensive bills failed in 2006–2007, 2010, 2013, and 2024; the 2012 DACA memorandum and the litigation that still limits it; and the contrasting uses of parole, asylum rules, Title 42, and INA section 212(f) under Obama, Trump, Biden, and the second Trump administration (47th presidency, inaugurated January 20, 2025).

Figures that can be tied to CRS, the Statutes at Large, USCIS, CBP, ICE, and the Supreme Court are labeled. Agency press claims and unfinished court cases are labeled too. A full Sources section points to Congress.gov, CRS Report R45020, CRS Report RS22111, USCIS, DHS, AP, and Reuters.

For students, journalists, new Hill staff, and any reader who wants the public-law spine rather than a campaign narrative.
